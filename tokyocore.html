What I did on TokyoCore

A survival SMP regularly holding 80+ players was dropping to 15 TPS. I had 14 days, a live server and no appetite for downtime. This is what I found, what I changed, and what I deliberately didn't change.

  • 14 dayscontract, delivered on time
  • 80+players at peak
  • Purpur / Leafserver software
  • 0changes made without the owner's approval

A harder test, same server. Two days in, I measured again at 86 players, when the server was carrying 97% more entities than at baseline because the farms had grown. TPS still rose from 15.73 to 17.03, and the slowest 5% of ticks went from 121 ms to 105 ms. Every change was re-verified on day 7 and again on day 14, and all were still in place.

What I did

  1. Measured first

    A full spark capture at 80 players and four hours of uptime: tick times, CPU, garbage collection, heap and an allocation profile. Every later decision was checked against it.

  2. Sized the heap properly

    The JVM was starting at 128 MB and allowed to grow to 26 GB, so it collected garbage every 3.8 seconds. A fixed 16 GB heap made collections four times rarer and cut their share of server time from 1.70% to 0.52%. Heap use fell from 12 GB to 4 GB.

  3. Found a silent failure, and repaired it

    Before I started, someone had edited the server's main performance config and left one line of invalid YAML. On the next restart the server couldn't read the file, quietly replaced it with defaults, and 34 optimisations were switched off with no visible error. I rebuilt the file from a clean, current copy, restored every lost setting, and checked 52 keys back from the server. That same session had also left behind a setting that logged 13 errors at every startup, which I removed.

  4. Fixed the villagers

    220 villagers were searching 48 blocks around them over and over, which made them nearly 5% of all memory allocation. Cutting the search radius to 16 and throttling it dropped villager ticking from 3.99% to 1.00%. They still trade exactly as before.

  5. Changed strategy when the server became CPU-bound

    Moving work onto other threads freed the main thread, until the 8 cores were fully used. From then on, moving work elsewhere would have made things worse, so I switched to removing work instead: trimming what the block logger records (water and lava flow, leaf decay, tree growth), capping how often inventories are backed up, throttling an effects library's refresh loop, and shrinking oversized thread pools.

  6. Followed the last stall to its real cause

    The remaining stalls at 80 players weren't the CPU. On the final day I traced them to disk I/O, and handed it over with the evidence rather than tuning settings that wouldn't have helped.

How I worked

Backed up, approved, verified

A verified backup came before the first change. Every change was proposed and cleared with the owner first, made as a targeted line edit with the comments kept, checked as valid YAML, uploaded, then read back from the server key by key. Every batch had a written rollback.

Gameplay changes aren't optimisations

The review list I was given included slowing spawners by about 50%, breaking entity pushing for farms, and turning off a reach check. All of them would have bought performance with the players' experience, so I said no to all of them. I also restored mob targeting another edit had made sluggish.

Read the comments, not the forum post

One planned setting turned out to be in milliseconds, not ticks, so as planned it would have done nothing. Another was in seconds, and changing it would have broken the feature behind it. Both were caught by reading the config before editing it.

Honest about what's left

The owner received a written log of every change, copies of every file before it was changed, and a list of open items that needed their decision: a plugin causing a freeze after each restart, a database purge that needs a maintenance window, and disk usage.
